Spec · landing page · 2026-08-11

Dark shell,
paper records

The page is dark because the work is: vans before light, lofts, plant rooms, a phone screen at 14:52. The only things that go light are the things that are records — the job sheet and the contact form. That split is the whole idea, and it's what stops it reading as a template.

Type

Archivo and Inter Tight were correct and characterless. The replacement is a heavy grotesk over an engineered workhorse. Chivo carries weight without shouting, and reads at a glance on a phone in daylight — the earlier condensed signage face looked the part but was hard work to read.

Chivo
Display · 600 / 700 / 900

A sturdy grotesk with weight and slightly squared terminals. Headlines, section heads, prices, quotes. Sentence case, tracking −0.02 to −0.035em, leading 1.0–1.2.

IBM Plex Sans
Body · 400 / 500 / 600

Engineered, slightly mechanical, holds up small. All running copy, form fields, list items.

IBM Plex Mono
Data · 400 / 500

Machine values and margin notes: job refs, bot handles, timestamps, section numbers, field labels. Never used for sentences that a person wrote to another person.

All three are SIL OFL and self-hostable. Ship woff2 subsets from your own origin — the CDN links in these prototypes are for preview only and must not survive into the build. Weights needed: Chivo 600/700/900, Plex Sans 400/500/600, Plex Mono 400/500. Roughly 180 KB total subset to Latin.

Measured with WCAG 2.1 relative luminance on the rendered sRGB values. Lowest text pair on the page is 5.2:1 (--text-3 on --raised); everything else clears 6:1. Nothing on the page relies on --line or --amber-fill for text contrast.

One rule to hold: amber never carries text on paper (2.1:1). On light surfaces amber is a fill or a 3px rule only, with --on-amber or --paper-ink sitting on it.

Imagery

Four photographs, one half-day with a pilot firm. Shoot on a real job, available light, no releases-signing models and no white studio backdrops. What we want to see is workwear, weather, and a phone being used with one hand.

01 · hero, landscape

Sparky mid-job, phone in hand, consumer unit open behind. Shot from the side, he isn't performing for the camera.

02 · portrait

Van doors open at first light, kit going in. Establishes the hour these people start.

03 · detail

Hands and a scuffed phone screen. Close enough to read that the phone is not new.

04 · reserve

Kitchen table, laptop, 9pm. The moment the paperwork used to happen. Holds the contact section if we want it.

Until then: the hatched amber plates in the prototype ship as-is. They are obviously placeholders, and an obvious placeholder is more honest than a stock photo of a smiling man in a clean hi-vis — this audience can spot that instantly, and it costs trust. Do not buy stock as an interim.

Layout & mobile

Every section is a numbered docket row: mono index, then a signage headline, then content. Rules are hairlines, corners are 4–6px. Nothing is centred except by accident — the page reads down the left edge like a form.

The prototype is fluid rather than breakpoint-driven: type is clamped and columns are flex 1 1 [min] pairs, so it reflows continuously from 360 to 1440 with no media queries. Drag the preview narrow to see the mobile layout. Two things to check in build: the nav collapses to logo + Sign in under about 520px, and the hero video sits above the fold on a phone — headline, one line of sub, then the film.

Tap targets in the form and the seat steppers are 44px minimum. Radii stay square-ish (4–6px) — that was the right instinct in the original system, it just needed the type and the hairlines to carry it.

Currency

The prototype shows £ — a UK owner reading $80 per person will assume this is an American product and price it wrong in his head. Prices are a tweak on the page if you need to switch back.

Fonts

Preview loads them from Google. Self-host woff2 before this goes anywhere near the CSP.

Amber's second job

In the app amber means attention required. On the landing it is the brand. Anyone crossing from one to the other twice a day will feel the collision — worth deciding whether the app keeps amber for alerts or moves to teal.

No social proof

Nothing faked. The three lines under the contact heading do the reassuring instead, and they say two pilot firms out loud.
